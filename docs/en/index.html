Schedule and patient records for a massage therapy clinic

clinic runs the front desk of a therapeutic massage clinic: the schedule of rooms and appointments, the patient records and the working time log of the therapists. It works on a single computer — no server, no cloud, no subscription. Patient data never leaves the workstation.

Why this program exists

A clinic with several rooms does not fit into an ordinary calendar

The clinic works in slots — a morning one and an afternoon one — and each slot has several rooms, each staffed by a different therapist. Every treatment is followed by a mandatory turnaround, an appointment may not reach beyond its slot, and at the end of the month somebody has to say who worked how many hours. An office calendar keeps none of that; clinic knows these rules and does not let them be broken.

A schedule that knows the rules

A day is divided into slots and a slot into rooms. The program watches the mandatory turnaround after a treatment, clashes in a room and in a patient's day, rooms taken out of service and closed days. Appointments are moved with the mouse, and a drop that is not allowed is shown while still dragging.

Patient records at the desk

Search by any fragment of any field, a journal of contraindications, a register of unexcused absences and a warning note — all visible before an appointment is saved. The appointments with the state of their invoices and a quick booking of the next one sit right in the patient card.

Working time that counts itself

Starting and finishing a slot opens and closes a log entry for every staffed room. A change of staffing in the middle of a slot splits the time between two people, corrections leave a trace, and the working time card and the summary go straight to paper or to PDF.

Functionality

What the program does

The screenshots below come from version 1.4.0 running on a sample, entirely invented set of data. Click a screenshot to enlarge it.

Calendar

The schedule of rooms and appointments

The main column is a day, the subcolumns are rooms and the rows are work slots. The height of a slot matches its length, so it is visible how much room time is really left.

  • Three spans — a day, several days (2–6) and a week (5 or 7 days), switched with one click or a shortcut.
  • The staffing heading above every room names everybody who worked in it during the slot; finished shifts are struck through and the colour comes from the therapist catalogue.
  • An appointment tile shows the time, the patient and the treatment, and once completed also the therapist; the colour and the opacity follow the status, an icon in the corner tells the invoice, and the turnaround is drawn as a separate bar.
  • Mouse work — selecting empty space creates an appointment, dragging moves it between hours, rooms and days, and the bottom edge changes its length.
  • Filters and scaling — hide statuses and rooms, zoom vertically and horizontally, print exactly what is on the screen.
Booking

Finding free times

The booking window takes a patient, a treatment, the number of treatments and a date range, and lists every time at which the treatment together with its turnaround fits. The ticked times become appointments with a single button.

  • A series of appointments — the “ticked k of N” counter makes sure the patient leaves with a full set, and clashes with their other appointments are blocked as they are ticked.
  • A horizon or an end date — the search reaches a number of days ahead or up to a day picked from a calendar; the window remembers the last choice and the last date.
  • Free rooms beside every time — either leave the choice to the program or pick a room by hand.
  • A therapist criterion — when the patient asks for somebody in particular, the result narrows down to the rooms those people staff.
  • Warnings before saving — the patient's contraindications, the untrusted mark and the months that have no schedule yet.
Patients

Patient records

The patient card holds the personal and address data, administrative notes, the journal of contraindications, referrals and consents, the register of absences and the full list of appointments with the state of each invoice.

  • One search field — first name, surname, record number, national ID, phone, e-mail or address; matching ignores letter case and Polish diacritics.
  • Contraindications as a journal — dated entries added one after another, never overwritten, with the warning already visible when the patient is picked.
  • Patient trust — unexcused absences are counted against a threshold; an untrusted patient is marked with a colour and an “(!)” everywhere they appear.
  • Appointments and invoices in one place — the “Appointments” tab gives the therapist and the state of the invoice for every appointment: no, yes or collected; a double click opens the appointment form.
  • Anonymisation on request (GDPR) — the personal data goes, the record number and the visit history stay.
  • The record number is assigned automatically and never reused.
Appointments

From booking to settlement

An appointment goes through its statuses: planned, confirmed, completed or cancelled. A therapist reaches the appointment only when it is marked as completed — and it is the person who was in the room at the moment the treatment started.

  • The appointment form with the length, the turnaround, the room, an administrative note and the invoice markers.
  • Any change of status — from every status to every other one, in any slot; a cancelled appointment comes back while its time is still free.
  • Cancelling with a reason and a “patient did not show up” box that feeds the register of absences.
  • Bulk completion of a whole slot once it has finished.
  • The call list — the appointments of a chosen day that nobody has confirmed yet, with phone numbers and confirmation right in the list.
  • Invoices — the program issues none, it only keeps track of the ones a patient has not collected yet.
Working time

Room staffing and the hours log

Counting starts the moment a slot is started and ends when it is closed — by hand or automatically. Every change of staffing closes one entry and opens the next, so the log describes presence in a room rather than a plan.

  • Staffing suggestions from the last staffed day or from the same weekday — to be confirmed, not guessed.
  • Substitutions and moves of staffing between rooms, with a single cutoff time and no gap in the log.
  • Corrections and manual entries — each of them marked, with the original hours kept.
  • A summary per person: days, staffed slots, total hours and lesson hours.
  • CSV export plus the printed working time card and the summary of the period.
Printouts

Reports for paper, for PDF or for the patient

One menu gathers every printout and one print window serves them all: paper, orientation, scaling, font size, date range and a page-by-page preview. The settings are remembered separately for each kind of report.

  • The patient's appointment card — dates and times only, without the room, the therapist or any note; it fits on A6.
  • The working time card of one therapist and the summary of a period — ready to be signed.
  • The daily admissions list — as one table or split into rooms.
  • The room staffing plan, the patient record and the calendar as it is shown on the screen.
  • PDF export for every report; internal printouts are clearly marked as such.
Settings

Catalogues and the clinic configuration

The program imposes no fixed numbers: the count of rooms and their names, the default slots of every weekday, the list of treatments and of therapists are all set by the clinic.

  • Therapists with their calendar colour, phone, active state and absences (holiday, sick leave).
  • Treatments with a length, a short name and an informative price.
  • Eight tabs of settings with a search field over them — from the clinic details to the status colours and the security rules.
  • Calendar — the scale and the starting span, the helper lines, and the colour, text size and height of the three headings of the grid; a taller strip wraps a long name instead of enlarging the letters.
  • Appearance — a light or dark theme or the one of the system, the widget style, the interface language (Polish or English) and the colours and opacity of the tiles, the colours of the patient markings and of the invoice icons.
Data and safety

Backups, the change log and GDPR

The data lives in XML files in the data directory — no SQL database and no server. The program takes care of the copies itself, and of being able to answer the question “who did this?”.

  • Backups automatically at start, periodically during work and — when set so — on exit; restoring from a list of copies, with a copy of the current state taken first.
  • The change log — date, user, kind of operation and record, with filters; it never holds the value from before a change, so that a second set of patient data cannot arise.
  • User accounts sign every operation with its author. By default they guard no access, but the clinic can close account creation in the sign-in window and require a password of every account — the way in is then an existing account and its password. The data files themselves stay for the workstation to protect.
  • A screen lock on Ctrl+L and after a period of inactivity.
  • GDPR — contraindications treated as a special category of data, anonymisation of a patient and of a therapist, and no patient data outside the data directory and its backups.

Download

Installing means unpacking an archive

Releases are published in GitHub Releases as ZIP archives — one for Windows and one for Linux. There is no installer, no administrator rights are needed, and the program runs from any directory it may write to.

The latest release

Current version: 1.6.0

Go to the releases on GitHub
  • Windows 10 / 11 · 64-bitA ZIP archive with the Qt libraries and the MSVC runtime
  • Linux · 64-bitBuilt on Ubuntu 22.04; HTTPS uses the system OpenSSL 3

Inside the archive: the clinic program, the updater, the Qt libraries, the translations, the manual in doc/clinic-manual.pdf and the licenses/ directory with the licence texts of the components.

The first run

  1. Unpack the archive into a directory the program may write to — for example C:\clinic or your home directory.
  2. Start clinic. On the first run it asks for the licence key; without one it starts in read-only mode.
  3. Sign in with any name — the account is created straight away and signs every operation from then on.
  4. Set up the clinic — its name, the rooms and the default slots in the settings window, then enter the therapists and the treatments.
  5. Create the schedule of the month (the Schedule menu) and start booking appointments.

Updates are built in. The program checks a signed manifest, downloads the archive, verifies its checksum and installs it with a separate updater — after a backup has been made and with the previous version still available to return to. The clinic data, the settings and the licence are left untouched.

Requirements

  • 64-bit Windows 10 or 11, or 64-bit Linux (glibc 2.35+, e.g. Ubuntu 22.04 and newer).
  • A screen of at least 1366 × 768; HiDPI scaling is supported.
  • Room for the data and the backups — tens of megabytes at a typical volume.
  • An internet connection only to fetch the licence and the updates; work without a network is possible thanks to the offline copy of the licence.
  • No server requirements at all — no database, no background service.

New in 1.6.0

  • The settings of the calendar have a “Calendar” tab of their own; “Appearance” keeps what the application as a whole is painted in, and the colours of the tiles and the markings.
  • Every heading of the grid has a strip height separate from its text size: making the strip taller buys room for a second line, not larger letters.
  • Room names and therapists' names are wrapped where the strip has room for it, instead of being cut off with an ellipsis.
  • A new setting for the size of the text on a tile.
  • The colour marking a patient — untrusted, or carrying a warning note — is now what the whole of the calendar tile is written in, exactly as the marking works on the lists.
  • The state of an invoice is read off an icon in the top right corner of the tile, on the line of the time: an outline while the patient waits for it, filled once it has been collected. The “[invoice]” words are gone and have given a line of the tile back.
  • Tile and text opacity — the colour picker of every status, of both invoice icons and of the patient markings has an alpha channel, and the tile text an opacity of its own.
  • Referrals and contact consents are added, edited and removed on the patient card; a withdrawn consent stays on the card as a date.
  • The status of an appointment changes to any other one, in any slot — “Mark as planned” is new, and a cancelled appointment comes back while its time is still free.
  • Fixed: the number of backups kept takes effect as soon as the settings are saved, without restarting the application.

The full list of changes is in the release notes on GitHub.

Licensing

The application licence and the third party components

The program is commercial software of its author and needs a valid licence to work in full. The libraries it uses are a separate matter — their licence texts are delivered with every release.

The application licence

The licence is a digitally signed file (Ed25519), downloaded from the licence server over HTTPS at every start and kept on the workstation as an offline copy. It names the licensee, the edition, the period of validity and the computer the program may run on.

  • One licence = one computer. The computer id is derived at every start and is never written to a file.
  • Working offline is possible — the program then uses the offline copy of the licence.
  • Without a valid licence the program starts in read-only mode: the data can be viewed, printed, exported and backed up, but not changed.
  • Updates work regardless of the state of the licence.
  • Replacing the computer means issuing the licence for the new id.

How to obtain a licence: open Help → About, copy the computer id with the button beside it and send it to krzysztof.kaczor.84@gmail.com. You will get back a licence key, which is entered in the program once.

Third party components

Beyond Qt and the C++ standard library the program uses no external libraries. The licence texts are part of the release archive (the licenses/ directory) and the About window names them.
